RESTORING ESSENTIAL SERVICES

Recovery priorities

  1. Protect people. Confirm site safety before handling equipment.
  2. Contain the incident. Assess the damage and prevent further disruption.
  3. Restore supporting infrastructure. Recover power, secure connectivity and essential server services.
  4. Restore business applications and data. Recover Sage ERP and other essential services according to technical dependencies and business impact.
  5. Verify and resume operations. Test restored services with the responsible departments before returning to normal use.

SCENARIOS & RESPONSES

Disaster scenarios

Proposed recovery actions relevant to AFC
Scenario Possible impact Recommended actions
Power failure Servers and network equipment stop working. Branches lose access to central services. Use UPS protection for a controlled shutdown. Use approved backup power where available. Check equipment and restore services safely after power stabilises.
Server or storage failure Sage ERP and central business data become unavailable. Assess the failure, repair or replace equipment, and restore systems from verified backups. Test data integrity and user access.
Ransomware or malware Systems may be encrypted or company information compromised. Isolate affected devices, notify the IT Manager and investigate the scope. Rebuild affected systems and restore verified clean backups before reconnecting.
Internet or VPN failure Branches cannot access Cape Town systems. Check local equipment and contact the provider. Use an approved backup connection if available. Test VPN connectivity and application access.
Fire, flooding or theft Equipment and locally stored backups may be damaged or lost. Prioritise evacuation and site safety. Arrange replacement equipment or an approved recovery location. Restore services using protected off-site backups.
Accidental data deletion Important business records are lost or altered. Identify affected data and restore an appropriate backup version. Check restored records with the responsible department before normal use resumes.

PROTECTING BUSINESS DATA

Backup arrangements

  • Back up critical business data daily, with the exact schedule agreed according to business needs.
  • Include Sage ERP data, required server configurations and network device configurations.
  • Use application-consistent database backups so that restored data remains usable.
  • Maintain three copies of important data across two storage types, with one copy off-site. Keep at least one backup offline or protected against alteration.
  • Encrypt backup copies and restrict access to authorised IT staff.
  • Check backup results daily and investigate failures promptly.
  • Agree retention periods with management and the departments responsible for the data.
  • Keep recovery instructions and essential contact details accessible during an outage.

COORDINATED RESTORATION

Recovery procedure

  1. Protect people and confirm that the location is safe.
  2. Report the incident to the IT Manager and record affected systems and business activities.
  3. Contain the problem and assess its extent before starting restoration.
  4. The IT Manager activates the appropriate recovery plan and obtains required approvals.
  5. Restore essential power, equipment, secure connectivity and supporting server services.
  6. Restore affected applications and data from verified backups, following technical dependencies.
  7. Test security, data integrity and application operation with the relevant department.
  8. Obtain approval to resume operations and inform affected employees.
  9. Monitor restored services and document outstanding problems.
  10. Review the incident and update the recovery plan.

AGREED & TESTED TARGETS

Recovery targets and testing

RTO
Recovery Time Objective: the target time for restoring a service after disruption.
RPO
Recovery Point Objective: how far back data recovery may go, indicating acceptable data loss measured in time.

Management must approve targets for each critical service based on business impact, available resources and cost.

Backup frequency must support the recovery target.

Daily backups alone may allow nearly 24 hours of data loss. A shorter RPO for Sage ERP requires more frequent application-consistent backups or another suitable protection method.

  • Conduct quarterly sample restoration tests, subject to management approval.
  • Conduct an annual coordinated recovery exercise, subject to management approval.
  • Verify that backups can be restored and staff understand their responsibilities.
  • Measure whether agreed recovery targets can be met.
  • Record results, failures and corrective actions.
  • Review the plan after major system changes and actual disasters.
